# Code of conduct

Bursar is built in public by people with different backgrounds, languages and levels of
experience. This document says how we expect everyone to treat each other while working on it.

## Where it applies

In this repository and every other repository under the bursar-world organisation: issues, pull
requests, reviews, discussions and commit messages. It also applies to Bursar's own channels,
such as the @UseBursar account on X, and to anyone representing the project elsewhere.

## What we expect

- Assume good faith. Most friction is a misunderstanding, and a question clears it faster than an
  accusation.
- Review the work, not the person. Say what is wrong with a change and why, and suggest what
  would fix it.
- Be patient with newcomers. Everyone was new to this codebase once.
- Accept a decision you disagree with once it has been made and explained. You can raise it again
  with new information.
- Credit other people's work, including ideas that came from a review or a discussion.
- Keep private what was shared privately, including security reports and personal details.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation or threats, in public or in private messages.
- Insults, slurs, or demeaning remarks about anyone's identity, background, body, ability or
  experience.
- Sexual language or imagery directed at a person.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as a home address, an email address or a wallet
  they have not made public, without their permission.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, repeated off-topic posts, or reopening settled
  arguments without new information.
- Promoting tokens, trading schemes or unrelated products in project spaces.
- Encouraging or rewarding any of the above.

## Reporting

Email **conduct@bursar.world**. Tell us what happened, where, when, and who was involved, with
links or screenshots if you have them. You can report something that happened to someone else.

The maintainers read every report and reply within a week. We keep the reporter's identity
private unless they agree otherwise. A maintainer involved in a report takes no part in handling
it.

## What happens next

Depending on what happened and whether it has happened before, the maintainers may:

1. contact the person privately to explain what was wrong and ask them to stop;
2. issue a public warning;
3. restrict them from commenting, opening issues or contributing for a stated period;
4. remove them from the project's spaces permanently.

We may remove, edit or hide comments, commits, code and issues that break this code of conduct,
whatever the outcome of a report.

## Changes

This document changes through a pull request, like any other file in the repository.
